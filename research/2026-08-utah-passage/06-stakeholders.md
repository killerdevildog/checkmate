# 06 — UTAH STAKEHOLDER AND OPPOSITION LANDSCAPE
### Operation Checkmate — who kills it, who carries it, and what it costs to buy neutrality
Research date: 2026-08-01. Target: 2027 General Session (convenes January 2027). Interim window: now through November 2026.

---

## 0. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT STRUCTURAL FACT

**Utah law enforcement does not oppose bills one agency at a time. It opposes them through one committee.**

The **Utah Law Enforcement Legislative Committee (ULELC)** — https://www.ulelc.org/ — exists specifically to "discuss, review, and provide feedback on bills that impact law enforcement." It meets **Mondays at 10:00 a.m.** Its nine member organizations are:

1. Utah Chiefs of Police Association
2. Utah Sheriffs' Association
3. Utah Statewide Association of Prosecutors (SWAP)
4. Utah Peace Officers Association
5. Utah Attorney General's Office
6. Utah Department of Public Safety (Commissioner)
7. Utah Department of Corrections (Executive Director)
8. Utah Department of Natural Resources (Law Enforcement Director)

(Source: https://www.ulelc.org/ — the site lists nine member organizations including the DNR law-enforcement director as a distinct seat.)

**Strategic consequence:** Operation Checkmate will not face eight separate opponents making eight separate arguments. It will face **one consolidated position, adopted in a Monday morning meeting, delivered by whichever member organization is most sympathetic to the committee it is testifying before.** Prosecutors testify to Judiciary. Sheriffs testify to rural members. Chiefs testify on the technical/operational objection.

**The corollary opportunity:** the ULELC is a *consensus* body. A bill that splits its membership — for example, one that increases prosecutor documentation duties (SWAP objects) while leaving patrol conduct untouched (Chiefs and Sheriffs indifferent) — does not get a unified ULELC position. **Every drafting decision below should be evaluated against one question: does this split the ULELC or unify it?** Operation Checkmate as currently drafted unifies it perfectly, because §1, §2, §5, §11 and §17 hit all nine members simultaneously.

The ULELC does not appear to publish its voting procedure or officers. **UNVERIFIED:** how positions are formally adopted (majority vote vs. consensus).

**Predicted committee testimony (verbatim-style):**

> "Mr. Chair, members of the committee — Utah FOP represents the men and women who answer the call at two in the morning. **Published positions:** The USA has taken formal positions opposing federal (USFS) law enforcement traffic enforcement on Utah state highways and county roads absent deputization by the elected county sheriff (https://downloads.regulations.gov/FS-2023-0012-0035/attachment_1.pdf), and favoring state-court over federal-court prosecution of drug and alcohol offenses. It testifies routinely — e.g., Sheriff Chad Jensen (then President) supported H.B. 334; Ryan Arbon spoke to S.B. 196 (Law Enforcement Agency Disclosure Amendments).

**Track record:** In 2024 Utah voters approved **Amendment C**, constitutionalizing the elected county sheriff (https://ballotpedia.org/Utah_Amendment_C,_Elections_of_County_Sheriffs_Amendment_(2024)). Sheriffs are the only law enforcement executives in Utah with a **constitutional** office and an independent electoral mandate. They are, politically, the most powerful law enforcement voice in rural Utah — which is where the Senate majority lives.

**Why the Sheriffs are Operation Checkmate's most dangerous opponent, more so than the FOP:** §9 (Citizen Council) creates citizen bodies "outside the judicial process" with power to **issue warrants and arrest**. To a Utah sheriff this is not a due-process reform. It is the creation of a parallel armed authority inside his county that he does not control and that the voters did not elect. This is the provision that turns polite opposition into a personal crusade.

**Predicted committee testimony:**

> "Members, I'm the elected sheriff of my county. Every four years my neighbors decide whether I keep this job — that's the accountability the Utah Constitution provides, and this body's own voters just reaffirmed it in Amendment C. Section 9 of this bill creates a council that isn't elected by anybody, that can compel production, issue a warrant, and make an arrest. I've read it three times and I cannot tell you who they answer to. I run a 22-deputy agency. I cannot indemnify my deputies against this. Please hold it."

**Neutralization note:** §9 is severable from everything else in the bill and is the single largest source of avoidable opposition. See §4.2.

### 1.5 The Utah Judiciary, the Administrative Office of the Courts, and the Utah State Bar

**This is the most nuanced relationship in the document, and the author is likely to misread it.**

**What happened in 2025.** The Republican supermajority ran a large package of bills constraining the judiciary, openly motivated by displeasure with 2024 rulings. The centerpiece was **HB 512** (House Majority Whip **Karianne Lisonbee**), creating a **Joint Legislative Committee on Judicial Performance** — appointed legislators who could evaluate hand-picked judges in public hearings and place a retention recommendation **directly on the ballot next to the judge's name**.

The response: **more than 900 attorneys signed a letter** opposing the package. The Utah State Bar formally opposed. Legal-community protests occurred. In early March 2025 legislative leaders and the judiciary announced a **truce/deal**; Lisonbee voluntarily halted HB 512.
- https://utahnewsdispatch.com/2025/02/26/a-broad-attack-utahs-judiciary-fights-bills-threatening-its-independence/
- https://utahnewsdispatch.com/2025/02/21/utah-bar-opposes-of-bills-flexing-legislative-influence-on-judiciary/
- https://www.kuer.org/politics-government/2025-02-26/utahs-legal-community-takes-a-stand-against-the-legislatures-push-for-court-reforms
- https://www.kuer.org/politics-government/2025-03-03/utah-lawmakers-call-a-truce-and-back-away-from-judicial-retention-reform-for-now
- https://utahnewsdispatch.com/2025/03/03/after-weeks-of-division-utah-legislative-leaders-announce-deal-with-judiciary/
- https://www.utahbar.org/legislature-wants-its-thumb-on-the-scale-in-judicial-retention-elections/

**What the Bar does institutionally.** By law and court rule the Bar takes positions **only** on matters related to the judiciary and the administration of justice, via its **Governmental Relations Committee** plus a vote of the **Bar Commission**, applying a *Keller*-based ("Keller-Pure") screen to stay within the constitutional limits on mandatory-bar political activity. In 2026 it supported HB 94, HB 260, SB 57, SB 182 and SJR 8; partially supported HB 517 (opposing only attorney-fee provisions) and SB 134 (with reservations); and **opposed 18 measures**: HB 262, 274, 366, 372, 392, 397, 424, 540, 600; HJR 5, 13, 21; SB 92, 233; SJR 5, 6.
- https://www.utahbar.org/utah-state-bar-position-on-package-of-bills-affecting-utah-courts/
- https://www.utahbar.org/utah-state-bar-takes-positions-on-additional-legislation-affecting-the-judiciary-access-to-justice/
- https://www.utcourts.gov/en/about/administration/judicial-council.html

**The two-edged reading:**

*The opening.* The Legislature is **already hostile to the judiciary** and has already been willing to legislate at it. §2.7 (Legislating from the Bench), §1.1's judicial offenses, and §17's judicial provisions are pushing on a door the Legislature has itself been shouldering. A legislator who ran HB 512 is not shocked by judicial accountability language.

*The trap.* The 2025 fight also proved that **the Utah legal establishment can mobilize 900 attorneys and the organized Bar in under three weeks, and that when it does, leadership folds.** Operation Checkmate as drafted — abolishing judicial immunity (§5.1), creating the offense of "Judicial Imposter," empowering citizen councils to review convictions (§9), voiding judicial instruments ab initio (§16), and **entrenching itself against amendment (§15)** — is a far broader attack on the judiciary than HB 512 was. It will draw the identical response, and it will draw it faster, because the mobilization infrastructure now exists and has a recent win.

**Predicted Bar testimony:**

> "The Bar takes positions only where the administration of justice is directly implicated, and this bill implicates it in every section. Section 5 removes judicial immunity. Section 9 authorizes non-judicial bodies to issue warrants and review convictions. Section 16 declares court orders void ab initio and criminalizes their enforcement. Section 15 purports to entrench this Act against future amendment by this body. Members — respectfully — a judge in this state would have to decide, in every contested case, whether ruling for one party exposes her personally to felony prosecution without any limitations period. That is not accountability. That is the elimination of the ability to decide. The Bar opposes."

**Also expect:** the **Administrative Office of the Courts** to produce a fiscal and operational impact statement, and the **Judicial Council** (chaired by the Chief Justice) to submit a formal response, as it did on jury-selection legislation.

### 1.8 Utah Association of Special Districts (UASD) and Utah School Boards Association (USBA)

**UASD** (https://www.uasd.org/) is the only statewide association representing all district types — including **fire protection, law enforcement, and municipal services districts**. It "closely monitor[s] every bill introduced," has direct legislator access, and **"participat[es] in coalitions with other local government organizations."** That last phrase is the operative one: UASD is a force multiplier for ULCT and UAC, not an independent actor. Note that UASD's own site hosts a Utah Local Governments Trust page (https://www.uasd.org/utah-local-governments-trust) — the insurance pool and the districts are institutionally intertwined.

**USBA** (https://usba.cc/) represents all 41 school districts, has a registered lobbyist with the Lt. Governor's office, and runs a **USBA/USSA Joint Legislative Committee that meets every Friday during session** (https://usba.cc/legislation/). USBA also provides **risk management** services to its members — meaning it, too, is an insurance-side actor.

**Relevance to Operation Checkmate:** These are second-tier opponents *unless* "Government Actor" as defined in §1 sweeps in school employees, SROs, and district officials — in which case they become first-tier and bring 41 school districts and every special district into the opposition. **The definition of "Government Actor" in §1 determines whether this bill has two local-government opponents or five.** That is a one-sentence drafting decision with enormous coalition consequences.

Also note: a Utah judge in 2025 ruled the public has a right to see records of a private education group that received millions in taxpayer funds (https://www.sltrib.com/news/education/2025/07/09/93-years-this-nonprofit-advised/) — evidence that Utah courts are currently receptive to transparency arguments against quasi-public bodies.

### 1.9 The Insurance Pools — Utah Local Governments Trust and Utah Counties Indemnity Pool

**This is where the bill actually dies, and most citizen drafters never see it coming.**

**Utah Counties Indemnity Pool (UCIP)** — https://ucip.utah.gov/about-us
- Interlocal reserve fund organized under the Utah Interlocal Cooperation Act **and the Utah Governmental Immunity Act**; operating since **January 1992**.
- Created because counties **could not obtain insurance in commercial markets** for their unique exposures at a stable price. **UCIP is not an insurance company — it is a government agency enabling self-insurance.**

**Why this is the kill mechanism.** Note the statutory dependency: **UCIP is organized under the Governmental Immunity Act itself.** The pools are priced against the UGIA's architecture:
- **63G-7-604** caps. Effective **July 1, 2024**: **$911,300** personal injury per person per occurrence; **$3,668,400** aggregate per occurrence; **$366,900** property damage. The State Risk Manager recalculates by CPI in even-numbered years by rule (R37-4). (https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter7/C63G-7-P6_1800010118000101.pdf ; http://utrules.elaws.us/uac/r37-4-2)
- **Notice of claim within one year** of accrual.
- **No punitive or exemplary damages** against governmental entities.
- **63G-7-902**: the entity **must defend and indemnify** its employee **unless** the case involves **fraud, malice, or the employee's use of alcohol or drugs** (https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter7/C63G-7-S902_1800010118000101.pdf). Where it declines, it must still provide coverage up to the 31A-22-304 amount.
- **63G-7-903**: entity recovery of judgments paid and defense costs (https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter7/C63G-7-S903_1800010118000101.pdf).

**Operation Checkmate removes the caps, removes the limitations period, adds retroactive civil liability (§10), and creates felony exposure — which, being "malice"-adjacent, falls into the 63G-7-902 exclusion and therefore is NOT indemnified.** The pools cannot price it. When an actuary cannot price a risk, the pool's answer is not "higher premium," it is **"excluded."**

**Predicted ULGT/UCIP posture:** They will likely not testify in their own name. They will supply the numbers to ULCT and UAC and to the Office of the Legislative Fiscal Analyst. **That is more dangerous than testifying**, because the fiscal note is the neutral-seeming instrument that kills bills.

**The fiscal note is the actual weapon.** Every Utah bill gets a fiscal note from the Office of the Legislative Fiscal Analyst, and Utah fiscal notes explicitly show impacts on **state agencies, local governments, individuals, and businesses** (https://budget.utah.gov/products/budgeting/ ; https://le.utah.gov/lfa/index.htm). LFA develops notes by **direct contact with affected agencies** — i.e., it will call ULCT, UAC, ULGT, UCIP, AOC and DPS. **Whatever those entities tell the analyst becomes the official price of the bill.** A bill carrying an indeterminate or nine-figure local-government fiscal note does not get a hearing in Utah. It does not need to be voted down.

**Action item for the author:** engage ULGT and UCIP **during the interim (now)**, not during session. The fiscal note is written before the first hearing.

## 3. DELIVERABLE 3 — THE COALITION PLAY

### 3.1 What the Utah record actually proves

Before designing a coalition, absorb the three cases that define the physics.

**Case 1 — HB 367 (2021), "Qualified Immunity Amendments," Rep. Brian S. King.** Would have allowed suit against officers and agencies for "unlawful, negligent or improper conduct" on duty. Introduced 2/16/2021. Fiscal note sent to sponsor 2/18/2021. **Enacting clause struck 3/5/2021.** Filed as a bill not passed. **It never received a committee hearing.** Contemporaneous reporting attributes this to the bill emerging late without stakeholder input.
- https://le.utah.gov/~2021/bills/static/HB0367.html
- https://legiscan.com/UT/bill/HB0367/2021

*Lesson:* a Democratic-sponsored immunity bill with no stakeholder pre-work in Utah does not get a hearing. It is not defeated. It is never heard. **Sponsor party and pre-session stakeholder work are the whole game.**

**Case 2 — the 2021 police reform package.** What passed: **SB 106** (POST model statewide use-of-force policy), **SB 13** (Sen. Jani Iwamoto — officers can't escape internal investigation by switching departments), **HB 162** (16 of 40 annual training hours on de-escalation and mental health), **HB 84** (use-of-force data reporting to BCI), **HB 334** (cadet training on autism and mental illness). What did **not** get hearings: **HB 237** (narrowing lethal force) and **HB 154** (defining when force may be used).
- https://www.kuer.org/politics-government/2021-02-09/utah-house-committee-approves-four-police-reform-bills
- https://www.deseret.com/utah/2021/2/9/22275010/flurry-of-measured-police-reform-bills-advance-utah-legislature-police-brutality-violence/
- https://www.deseret.com/utah/2021/3/16/22334514/utah-council-tackles-officer-discipline-preps-to-enact-new-police-reform-laws-post/
- ACLU/LWV 2021 scorecard (link now 404s; cited in coverage above)

*Lesson:* **the bills that passed were the ones law enforcement supported.** Training, data, decertification portability — all passed. Anything constraining an officer's conduct in the moment — never heard. The dividing line is not left/right. It is *does the ULELC object.*

**Case 3 — SB 262 (2026), Sen. Dan McCay (R-Riverton).** Would have barred unmarked vehicles from making infraction-level traffic stops. Modest. Republican-sponsored. Libertas-endorsed. Grounded in a sympathetic constituent story (a woman stopped at night in a remote area by a man in jeans and a polo shirt). **Passed Senate Judiciary, Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice 6–1. - https://www.ksl.com/article/51451607/utah-lawmakers-target-police-quotas-unmarked-cop-cars

*Lesson — and this is the sobering one.* A Republican senator, a libertarian think tank, a sympathetic victim narrative, a 6–1 committee vote, and a bill about **paint on cars** — and organized law enforcement opposition still killed it on the floor **five months ago**. **Synthesis: in Utah, a police-accountability bill passes if and only if organized law enforcement is neutral. Not supportive — neutral. Neutrality is the product, and it is purchased with drafting.**

### 3.2 The two-organization answer

**If exactly two organizations jointly endorsed a narrowed Operation Checkmate, the pair that makes it close to unstoppable is the Libertas Institute and the Utah League of Cities and Towns.**

Not the intuitive answer, so here is the reasoning:

- **Libertas supplies what no one else can: a credible Republican sponsor and conservative intellectual cover.** It has an eleven-bill police-reform track record in Utah (below), a president with fifteen years of relationships, and first-in-nation wins. In a 23R/6D Senate and 61R/14D House, the reform must be *conservative-coded* or it does not get heard — see HB 367.
- **ULCT supplies the disappearance of the fiscal note.** ULCT is the only entity that can tell the Office of the Legislative Fiscal Analyst that the local-government impact is manageable. As long as ULCT is opposed, the bill carries a catastrophic or indeterminate local-government fiscal note and never reaches a floor vote. ULCT neutrality is not a nice-to-have; it is the difference between a bill and a press release.

Libertas alone gets you SB 262 — a good bill that dies on the floor. Libertas plus ULCT gets you a bill that survives the floor.

**The third leg is ULELC neutrality**, which is bought entirely in §4 below and is the actual hard work.

### 3.3 Where the ACLU of Utah fits — and a caution

The left-right pairing is genuinely how Utah criminal justice reform passes, and the ACLU of Utah should be in the coalition. But two cautions the author should hear:

1. **The ACLU is a decarceration organization.** Operation Checkmate creates well over a hundred new criminal offenses — roughly 53 umbrella offenses in §1 plus roughly 70 role-specific offenses in §17, with felony penalties and no limitations period. Structurally, that is a **mass-criminalization bill**, and the fact that the defendants are officials does not fully resolve the ACLU's institutional objection to expanding the criminal code, eliminating limitations periods, and creating retroactive liability. Expect the ACLU to support the *civil* and *due-process* provisions enthusiastically and to be quiet or uncomfortable on the criminal track.
2. **ACLU endorsement is double-edged in the Utah House.** Lead with Libertas as the public face; bring the ACLU in as a named supporter at the floor stage and for the left-flank press, not as the bill's front. This is a sequencing point, not a slight.

### 3.4 The narrowed bill that unites Libertas and the ACLU of Utah

**Working title: the Utah Government Accountability and Documentary Integrity Act.**

The unifying principle — and it is a genuinely powerful one — is: **liability attaches to what a government actor wrote, swore, or filed, not to what they decided in a fraction of a second.** Both organizations can sign that sentence. Neither can be accused of second-guessing a life-or-death judgment call. And it happens to capture the conduct that actually produces wrongful convictions.

### 3.5 The single most important structural insight in this document

**The criminal track splits the coalition. The civil-and-professional-sanction track unites it.**

Operation Checkmate's theory does not actually require new felonies. Nearly every core wrong it targets is **already** a Utah crime — falsifying a government record, tampering with evidence, perjury, official misconduct. What is missing in Utah is not a *prohibition*. It is a **remedy the citizen can invoke and an immunity doctrine that doesn't swallow it.**

Convert the enumerated offenses from *new crimes* into a package of:
1. a **civil cause of action** with defined elements,
2. **mandatory professional consequence** (POST decertification, Bar referral, disqualification from future office), and
3. a **mandatory-referral duty** to an independent prosecutor for conduct that already violates existing criminal law,

and you achieve substantially the same deterrent while:
- gaining the ACLU (no new carceral apparatus),
- gaining Libertas (no overcriminalization; they have written against exactly this),
- shrinking the fiscal note by an order of magnitude,
- removing the Bar's strongest objection,
- and removing the FOP's most sympathetic talking point ("you are making my members felons for paperwork").

**This one restructuring does more for passage probability than every other recommendation in this document combined.**

### 3.6 The Spackman hook — the cheapest high-value provision available

Under **Spackman ex rel. Spackman v. Board of Education, 2000 UT 87**, damages are available for a Utah constitutional violation only where (1) the provision is self-executing, (2) the plaintiff suffered a **"flagrant"** violation, (3) existing remedies do not redress the injury, and (4) equitable relief is wholly inadequate. Utah courts have construed **"flagrant"** to require violation of **"clearly established" rights "of which a reasonable person would have known"** — i.e., **Utah has imported a qualified-immunity-style standard into its own state constitutional damages remedy by judicial construction.**
- https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ut-supreme-court/1490521.html
- https://law.justia.com/cases/utah/supreme-court/2000/spackman.html

**Because "flagrant" is a judicial gloss and not a constitutional command, the Legislature can define it by statute.** A short bill providing that, for an enumerated category of conduct (knowing falsification of a government record; knowing use of false evidence; a search or seizure conducted without articulable lawful authority), the violation **is flagrant as a matter of law**, accomplishes a large fraction of §5's purpose:

- It carries **no fiscal note of consequence** — it does not touch the UGIA caps, the notice-of-claim period, or the limitations period.
- It creates **no new crime**.
- It is **three pages**, not 7,900 lines.
- It is framed as **restoring** the remedy the Utah Constitution's framers assumed, not creating a new one — the *Spackman* court itself grounded the remedy in common-law history.
- It is defensible to a conservative committee as **judicial-activism correction**, which is a live and popular frame in this Legislature after 2025.

**Recommendation: this should be the author's first bill.** It is the highest ratio of substantive effect to political cost anywhere in the document.

## 4. DELIVERABLE 4 — NEUTRALIZATION STRATEGY

The objective is not fairness. It is the **minimum concession that buys the maximum passage probability**. For each opponent below: the concession, the drafting instruction, and what it buys.

### 4.1 Utah FOP and the law enforcement rank-and-file → NEUTRAL

**The prompt's hypothesis is correct.** Exempting split-second good-faith conduct while retaining liability for documentary falsification is very close to the only concession that neutralizes the FOP, and it does neutralize it.

**Drafting instruction — the Deliberation Line:**

> **Safe harbor.** No liability arises under this chapter from a decision to use force, to pursue, or to detain, made during a rapidly evolving encounter in which the actor lacked a meaningful opportunity for deliberation. Such decisions shall be evaluated from the perspective of a reasonable officer on the scene, without the benefit of hindsight.
>
> **No safe harbor.** Nothing in this section limits liability for any statement, report, affidavit, charging document, evidence submission, or record created, altered, omitted, or submitted **after** the encounter, or for any sworn statement whenever made.

Why this works, in the FOP's own terms: it protects the thing officers genuinely fear (being second-guessed on a two-second decision by someone with a year to think about it) and abandons only the thing no officer will publicly defend (lying on paper afterward). **A union cannot testify against a bill that punishes falsifying reports without appearing to defend falsifying reports.** In testimony, that asymmetry is decisive.

**Three additional cheap concessions that finish the job:**
- **Delete §11's automatic forfeiture of earned retirement benefits.** Substitute POST decertification (existing machinery) plus disqualification from future office. Automatic benefit stripping without a hearing is what turns the FOP *and* UPEA from negotiable into implacable, and it is not load-bearing.
- **Restore a limitations period.** Use a **discovery rule** — the period runs from when the falsification was or reasonably should have been discovered. This captures concealed misconduct (the actual concern) while removing "this bill has no statute of limitations, ever" from every opposing witness's opening line.
- **Preserve and expand Utah Code § 63G-7-902's defend-and-indemnify duty** so the honest officer is never personally exposed, with § 63G-7-903 recovery from the individual **only** on a finding of knowing falsification. This lets the sponsor say, truthfully: *"This bill makes your city defend you. It reaches your wallet only if you lied."*

**Buys:** FOP → neutral. Chiefs → neutral. UPEA → neutral or supportive. Costs almost nothing the bill's theory requires.

### 4.2 Utah Sheriffs' Association → NEUTRAL

**Concession: delete §9's Citizen Council arrest, warrant, and indictment powers.**

This is non-negotiable if the author wants the bill heard. The Sheriffs hold a **constitutional** office reaffirmed by the voters in Amendment C (2024), and §9 creates an unelected body with arrest and warrant authority inside their jurisdiction. There is no version of the Sheriffs' Association tolerating that.

**What to substitute, which loses less than it appears:**
- A **Citizen Oversight Council** with **subpoena/document-compulsion power** and **public reporting** — real teeth, no arrest power.
- A **mandatory-referral mechanism**: where the Council finds probable cause, the matter goes to a **designated independent prosecutor** (e.g., a conflict-county attorney or an independent unit) with a **duty to act and a duty to publish written reasons for declination.** The published-declination duty is the accountability engine; the arrest power was never what made §9 work.

**Buys:** Sheriffs → neutral. This is also the concession that most improves the bill's reception among rural senators, who are the hardest bloc in the Legislature.

### 4.3 SWAP → "CONCERNS," NOT "OPPOSED"

Full neutrality is unrealistic. Downgrading from active opposition to registered concerns is achievable and sufficient.

**Concession 1 — absolute protection for charging discretion.** No liability for a decision to charge or to decline. Liability only for: knowing suppression of exculpatory evidence; knowing use of false or fabricated evidence; charging in retaliation for constitutionally protected activity. **All three are already duties** under *Brady* and Rule 3.8 — the bill supplies a remedy, not a new obligation. That framing is the entire argument.

**Concession 2 — enact SWAP's own words and the Legislature's own auditor's.** SWAP has publicly stated that it is a **best practice to include the reasoning for each plea deal within the case file** (https://www.swap-ut.org/). The Office of the Legislative Auditor General found in 2025 that the Salt Lake County DA's office lacked documentation to analyze plea deals and lacked clearly defined filing policies, calling it "a leadership shortfall." A bill codifying written charging and plea rationale is **SWAP's stated standard plus the Legislature's own audit finding**. Testifying against it requires SWAP to repudiate its own best-practice statement in a public hearing.
- https://www.ksl.com/article/51416846/audit-calls-for-more-transparency-in-how-salt-lake-county-da-makes-decisions
- https://kutv.com/news/2news-investigates/lawmakers-pounce-as-salt-lake-county-district-attorney-fails-to-show-for-audit-results

**Concession 3 — rural capacity.** Many Utah counties run one or two prosecutors; "you will break small counties" is SWAP's strongest and most honest argument. Attach a **small-county prosecutor support appropriation** or a phased compliance schedule for counties under a population threshold. This converts rural county attorneys from opponents into beneficiaries and defuses the argument before it is made.

**Timing note:** SWAP's **Executive Director position is currently listed as vacant**. An association without a full-time ED is slower and more reactive. **This window will close.** Approach during the 2026 interim.

### 4.4 ULCT + the insurance pools (ULGT / UCIP) → NEUTRAL

**This is the concession set that determines whether the bill gets a fiscal note it can survive.**

**Concession 1 — keep the Governmental Immunity Act architecture. Legislate inside it, not around it.** Place the new cause of action within Title 63G Chapter 7 and **preserve**: the § 63G-7-604 caps (currently **$911,300** per person / **$3,668,400** aggregate / **$366,900** property, effective July 1, 2024, CPI-adjusted by the State Risk Manager in even-numbered years under R37-4), the one-year notice of claim, and the bar on punitive damages against entities. The pools are actuarially built on these numbers — **UCIP is literally organized under the Governmental Immunity Act.** Remove them and the risk becomes unpriceable, and unpriceable risk is **excluded**, not surcharged.

**Concession 2 — delete §10 (retroactive civil applicability) entirely. Prospective only.** *Highest value-per-unit-of-substance-lost in the entire bill.* Retroactive claims are a small fraction of the bill's future deterrent effect and **100% of the actuarial objection.** An actuary can price a prospective, capped, defined-element cause of action. No actuary on earth can price unlimited retroactive liability, and the ULGT/UCIP response to "cannot price" is a coverage exclusion — which becomes ULCT's testimony that small towns will disband their police departments.

**Concession 3 — the state indemnification backstop, which is the prompt's hypothesis and it is correct.** Create a **state-administered fund** (Division of Risk Management) that indemnifies local entities for judgments under the new chapter above the entity's pool retention. This converts ULCT's testimony from *"this bankrupts my towns"* to *"the state has assumed the risk."* Fund it visibly — civil penalties recovered under the Act, and forfeiture proceeds — so the fiscal note shows an **offsetting revenue line** rather than a naked cost. A fiscal note with a revenue offset is a fundamentally different political object than one without.

**Concession 4 — narrow the "Government Actor" definition in §1** so it does **not** automatically sweep in school districts and special districts. This is one sentence, and it determines whether the author faces two organized local-government opponents or five (adding USBA's 41 school districts and UASD's every-district-in-the-state coalition).

**Process instruction — act now, not in January.** ULCT sets positions through a **Legislative Policy Committee that is a noticed public body** (https://www.ulct.utah.gov/advocacy/legislative-policy-committee/ ; https://www.utah.gov/pmn/sitemap/publicbody/5483.html). **Attend it during the 2026 interim.** Separately, contact ULGT and UCIP directly. **The Office of the Legislative Fiscal Analyst builds fiscal notes by direct contact with affected agencies** (https://le.utah.gov/lfa/lfabkgd.htm) — meaning ULCT, UAC, ULGT and UCIP write the bill's price tag **before the first hearing**. Whatever they believe in December 2026 becomes the official cost in January 2027.

**Buys:** ULCT → neutral. Pools → silent. Fiscal note → survivable. This is the difference between a bill and a press release.

### 4.5 The Judiciary, the AOC, and the Utah State Bar → OUT OF THE FIGHT ENTIRELY

The Bar takes positions **only** on matters affecting the judiciary and the administration of justice, screened by its Governmental Relations Committee and Bar Commission under a *Keller*-based test. **That mandate is a jurisdictional limit — and it can be drafted around.** Strip the provisions that implicate the judiciary directly and much of the bill falls **outside** the Bar's position-taking authority. That is a procedural win, not a rhetorical one.

**Concessions, in descending order of necessity:**
1. **Delete §15's entrenchment clause.** A statute purporting to bar its own future amendment converts institutional caution into existential opposition, invites an immediate structural challenge, and is unenforceable against a future Legislature anyway. It buys nothing and costs everything.
2. **Delete §15's supremacy-over-federal-law provision.** Reframe as Utah providing protections **greater than the federal floor** — unremarkable, well-precedented state constitutional law, and precisely what the *Spackman* line already does. Same substance, none of the reaction.
3. **Remove judicial immunity abolition (§5.1 as to judges) and the "Judicial Imposter" offense from the first bill.** Judicial accountability is the single hardest lift in Utah and it poisons every provision attached to it. Substitute: a **judicial-conduct referral duty**, **published reasons**, and **recusal disclosure** — real transparency, no immunity fight.
4. **Delete §16's criminalization of issuing or enforcing a void instrument.** Retain void-ab-initio as a **civil defense and a bar to enforcement**. Do not make a felon of the clerk who docketed the order.
5. **Delete §9 entirely** (already required by §4.2). The Bar's sharpest objection is a non-judicial body reviewing convictions and issuing warrants.

**Why this matters more than it appears.** In 2025, **900+ attorneys** mobilized against the judicial package in under three weeks, and legislative leadership — a Republican supermajority at the height of its confidence — **folded and announced a truce.** That infrastructure exists, it is warm, and it has a recent win. Operation Checkmate as drafted is a substantially broader intervention in the judiciary than HB 512 was. **Do not fight the 2025 fight again on worse ground.**

**The counterintuitive corollary — there is a real opening here.** The same Legislature that folded is still institutionally irritated with its judiciary. Provisions framed as **correcting judicial overreach** — most importantly the *Spackman* "flagrant" definition (§3.6), which literally overrides a judicial gloss that narrowed a citizen remedy — can be sold to this Legislature as judicial-activism correction. **Attack the doctrine, not the judges.** Same result, no 900-attorney letter.

### 4.6 What NOT to concede

Concession is a budget, not a virtue. Spend it only where it buys neutrality. **Hold these, because none of them carries a meaningful fiscal note and none triggers the ULELC's core interest:**

- **§1.1.48's articulable-authority rule and evidentiary presumption.** Cheap, profound, and philosophically irresistible to a Utah Republican: *government must be able to state the authority for what it does to you.*
- **The documentary-integrity core** — falsification, evidence fabrication, forensic corruption, record distortion. This is the coalition's keystone. Everything else is negotiable; this is the bill.
- **The Act-wide post-hoc-justification doctrine** (extracted in §1.1.50 per the repo's v5.8.23 history). Pairs perfectly with the articulable-authority rule and costs nothing.
- **Recording rights and anti-retaliation.** Libertas already passed SB 239 (2017); this is expanding a Utah law that exists.
- **Transparency and published-declination duties.** These ride the AG-audit and DA-audit news cycles.
- **The *Spackman* "flagrant" statutory definition.** The highest-leverage provision in the entire document.

# Email - U.S. Commission on Civil Rights

Template for contacting the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights about **Operation Checkmate**, possible civil-rights concerns involving probation speech restrictions, and the need for federal and state-level government-accountability legislation. Replace bracketed placeholders before sending.

Suggested recipient: `referrals@usccr.gov`

Optional additional recipient for policy/public affairs questions: `publicaffairs@usccr.gov`

---

**Subject:** Request for civil-rights referral and policy review: Operation Checkmate and speech restrictions affecting advocacy

---

Dear U.S. Commission on Civil Rights,

My name is [Your full name], and I am writing as a private citizen from Utah to request civil-rights referral, review, and policy attention concerning a proposed government-accountability bill called **Operation Checkmate**.

Operation Checkmate is a citizen-written proposal intended to make government officials accountable under the same law as ordinary citizens when they misuse public power. The bill is aimed at government corruption, official retaliation, selective non-enforcement, obstruction, unlawful immunity shields, coercive process, wrongful convictions, abuse in custody, poverty-based enforcement, and other patterns where citizens are harmed by public authority but have little practical recourse.

The core principle is simple: public power is a public trust. A badge, robe, office, agency title, or government position should not place anyone above the law.

I believe there should be both **federal-level** and **state-level** versions of this bill. A federal version could address federal actors, federal civil-rights enforcement gaps, federal funding conditions, and national standards for official accountability. State versions could address state, county, municipal, court, law-enforcement, correctional, child-welfare, school, regulatory, and local-government misconduct. The state-by-state approach matters because many of the most direct abuses citizens experience happen through local systems.

I am contacting the Commission because the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights is an independent, bipartisan agency that informs the development of national civil-rights policy and monitors federal civil-rights enforcement. I understand that the Commission does not act as my personal advocate or directly enforce individual claims. Still, I am asking for referral to any appropriate office, review by any relevant state advisory committee or policy staff, and consideration of whether the issues raised by Operation Checkmate deserve investigation, study, hearing, reporting, or recommendation.

I am also requesting attention to a specific barrier affecting my ability to advocate for this bill.

As I understand my current probation requirements, I am not allowed to use social media. This restriction was imposed through proceedings involving **Judge [full name / exact spelling, currently believed to be Judge Windward]** in the State of Utah, case number **[case number]**. Because modern public advocacy, legislative organizing, petitioning, public education, and citizen participation often happen through social media, this restriction substantially limits my ability to speak publicly about Operation Checkmate, build support, contact communities, answer questions, and advocate for civil-rights legislation.

I am not asking the Commission to give me legal advice or represent me. I am asking whether this situation should be referred for civil-rights review or investigation, including whether a probation condition that restricts social-media use may unlawfully burden speech, petitioning, association, or civic participation when applied to advocacy for government-accountability reform.

The reason this matters is bigger than my own situation. If citizens who have experienced government power are restricted from publicly discussing reform, then the public loses access to the very testimony and organizing needed to identify and correct civil-rights failures. Operation Checkmate is intended to create lawful, peaceful accountability mechanisms before distrust, institutional failure, and official misconduct produce deeper social damage.

I respectfully ask the Commission to consider:

1. Referring my concern about probation-related speech restrictions to the appropriate civil-rights or legal office;
2. Reviewing Operation Checkmate as a proposed model for government accountability and civil-rights enforcement;
3. Considering whether federal and state-level versions of this bill could help close gaps in current civil-rights enforcement;
4. Sharing this issue with any appropriate state advisory committee, policy staff, congressional-affairs staff, or public officials who may be able to study or support this effort;
5. Advising where a private citizen should send supporting documentation, including the probation order, case information, and the current bill draft.

# Generic Outreach Email — Operation Checkmate

A general-purpose pitch you can adapt and send to anyone who might be interested in a government accountability bill: legislators, journalists, attorneys, commentators, activists, faith leaders, business owners, veterans, neighbors, family. The text below is written to work without modification for most recipients. Use the **customization checklist** to tailor it where you want a sharper fit.

---

## Customization checklist (read before sending)

1. **Subject line.** Default works for most recipients. If the recipient has a specialty (police accountability, civil rights litigation, civic education, veterans issues, etc.), consider swapping in a subject line that names it.
2. **Salutation.** Replace `[NAME]` with the recipient's name. If you are unsure how to address them, "Hello," works; for formal recipients, "Dear [Mr./Ms./Title] [Last name],".
3. **Opening sentence.** The template says "I'm a private citizen in Utah." Adjust the location if you live elsewhere. If you have a brief connection to the recipient (you watch their channel, you live in their district, you read their book), one short sentence acknowledging that lands much better than no acknowledgment.
4. **Optional case-citation paragraph.** Marked clearly in the body. **Delete it for non-lawyer recipients** — most citizens don't need the case names. **Keep it for attorneys, law students, judges, journalists who cover legal substance**, or any recipient who would find specific case citations clarifying rather than alienating.
5. **The ask.** Default ask is *read, share, support*. If the recipient is in a position to do something specific — sponsor the bill (legislator), cover it (journalist/commentator), endorse it (organization), introduce it in their state (citizen organizer in another state), file an issue (developer/citizen contributor) — replace the generic ask with the specific one.

## Email

**Subject:** A citizen-written state accountability bill — built as a model for every state — and an ask for your read

[NAME],

I'm a private citizen in Utah. Over the last several months I've been drafting a state accountability bill called **Operation Checkmate**, and I'd like to ask you to read it. It is a real, fully drafted statutory proposal — not a press release, not a manifesto, not talking points — and it exists in the open on GitHub precisely so that citizens, attorneys, journalists, educators, legislators, and anyone else who would have something to contribute can read it, criticize it, and improve it.

I'm bringing it to you because the case I want to make is, fundamentally, a case about **how America heals**.

**The premise.** America is divided in part because, for several decades now, government has been allowed to operate under a different set of rules than the citizens it governs. A judge who orders unlawful force keeps the robe. A prosecutor who knowingly uses false testimony keeps the office. An officer who allegedly steals during a search keeps the badge. The same conduct, done by an ordinary citizen, would lead to prosecution within days. The asymmetry is the daily experience of every American who has been on the receiving end of it — Black families ground down by police violence and qualified immunity, conservatives and federalists ground down by federal overreach, working-class people ground down by predatory municipal fines, LGBTQ+ citizens ground down by selective enforcement, veterans ground down by VA mistreatment, small business owners ground down by retaliatory regulators. The mechanism that produced every one of those wounds is the same. And when people on opposite sides of every political fight all conclude that the system does not apply the law to itself — they stop trusting each other, because the only thing they have in common is the experience of being failed by the same broken architecture.

**Operation Checkmate heals that division by closing the architecture that created it.** It does not ask Americans to agree on policy, on culture, on identity, on which party to vote for, or on which side of any current fight is right. It asks for one thing: that the same rules apply to everyone, regardless of office. When the rules apply to everyone the same way, the people who have been fighting each other discover that they were never each other's actual problem. The actual problem is the architecture that has exempted the people in power from the rules of the game everyone else lives by. Remove that exemption, and the country has a basis to reunite around — not a political consensus, but a constitutional one: **no person, office, agency, badge, robe, title, or institution stands above the citizen whose rights government exists to protect.**

**Two metaphors that have proven useful when explaining the bill.**

**The first is chess.** *Checkmate* is the position in which the **king** — the highest-ranking piece on the board, the one the whole game is organized around — has been placed in check, **and has no lawful move left to escape it**. The king isn't removed by trickery, assassinated, or overthrown. The king is brought to a stop by the lawful rules of the game, openly and in full view of both players. The match ends because the king is finally, demonstrably, bound by the rules like every other piece on the board. That is precisely the posture this bill places officials in. Judges shielded by absolute judicial immunity, prosecutors shielded by absolute prosecutorial immunity, officers shielded by qualified immunity, agencies shielded by sovereign immunity — none of them are removed from the board by this bill. They are simply, finally, bound by the same rules that already bind every other piece on it. The citizen makes the move. The rules of the constitutional order do the rest. **That is what healing looks like at the structural level.** It is not a victory of one side over another. It is the restoration of a game in which the rules apply to everyone.

**The second is the discipline gap.** America runs three regimes of authority, and only two of them have a real disciplinary code. The **military** is disciplined under the **Uniform Code of Military Justice** — a comprehensive criminal code specific to the exercise of military power, in which the foundational principle is that **rank does not exempt anyone from the law**. A captain who falsifies a report answers for it. A colonel who abuses a subordinate answers for it. **Citizens** are disciplined under the **ordinary criminal code** — every state's penal title. Citizens who steal go to court. Citizens who assault someone go to court. Citizens who fabricate evidence go to court. The third regime — **the civilian government itself, when it exercises power over citizens stateside** — has no parallel. The closest analogues are 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and a thin patchwork of state misconduct statutes, hollowed out by the immunity doctrines and by the practical reality that prosecutors do not charge their own. **Operation Checkmate is the disciplinary code the third regime never received.** It does not invent new principles. It applies, to the civilian government, the principle the military has lived under for decades and that citizens have lived under for centuries.

The bill abrogates the immunity doctrines case by case in its substantive text. Section L cites the doctrinal anchors by name: *Bradley v. Fisher* (foundational judicial immunity), *Pierson v. Ray*, *Stump v. Sparkman* (judicial immunity applied to a judge who approved the sterilization of a 15-year-old without a hearing), *Mireles v. Waco* (judicial immunity applied to a judge who ordered force against a public defender), *Imbler v. Pachtman* (absolute prosecutorial immunity even for knowingly using false testimony), *Harlow v. Fitzgerald* (the "clearly established law" qualified-immunity test), and *Jessop v. City of Fresno* (officers shielded by qualified immunity in an alleged theft during a search). Each is matched to the specific provision of the Act that closes the doctrinal hole it opened.

**This is written as a model bill — meant to be passed in every state.** Utah is where I live and where I have standing to push. But every substantive provision is drafted to be portable. **Your state can adopt this.** The doctrinal abrogations track federal Supreme Court immunity cases that apply nationwide. The substantive offenses describe government misconduct patterns that occur in every state. The Citizen Council enforcement mechanism — twelve or more citizens convened jointly, with the power to investigate and find — is built on a universal principle. If you would like to see this bill adopted in your state, **please email citizens.checkmate@gmail.com with subject `CHECKMATE — [STATE NAME]`** and a state-specific branch of the repository will be set up for that state. State-specific branches are coordinated centrally — under the same canonical project — so the work done in one state strengthens the work in every other, and so the model stays consistent and improvable across jurisdictions.

**The ask.**

1. **Read the bill** — or, if the full statutory text is more than you have time for, read the README and the long-form FAQ, which are written in plain language for citizens, not lawyers.
2. **Tell me what you think.** What's wrong, what's weak, what's worth strengthening, what's missing entirely. Pull requests, GitHub issues, or a direct email back — any format works.
3. **If you support what it's trying to do, please share it.** With whoever in your circle would want to know it exists. With your representatives. With any platform you have. A citizen-written bill becomes a real bill the same way any citizen-led reform has ever become a real bill — through the patient, distributed, ground-level work of citizens who decided not to wait for someone else to do it for them.

**Everything you need is in one place:**

- Repo: https://github.com/killerdevildog/checkmate
- README: https://github.com/killerdevildog/checkmate/blob/master/README.md
- Long-form FAQ: https://github.com/killerdevildog/checkmate/blob/master/faq.md
- Latest PDF (uploadable to ChatGPT/Claude/Grok if you want to interrogate it with an AI before reading the full text — well under 1 MB): https://github.com/killerdevildog/checkmate/releases/latest

This is a citizen-written bill, not a lobbyist product. No PAC, no consultants, no donor class behind it. It is explicitly non-partisan — the immunity architecture and the conduct it shields are bipartisan failures, and the citizens harmed by them span every political identity. The bill applies the same standard to every government actor regardless of party, office, or rank, in the same spirit the UCMJ applies the same standard to every service member regardless of branch, billet, or insignia.

Thank you for your time. The country needs more citizens willing to engage with this kind of work, and the fact that you opened this email is part of how that engagement happens.

## Notes on adaptation

- **For legislators**, swap the third item in *The ask* for a sponsorship request: *"If you find the architecture sound, please consider introducing it — in substantially this form, or in adapted form for your state — and let me know what assistance from the citizen author would be useful."*
- **For journalists and commentators**, swap the third item for a coverage ask: *"If you find it sound, please consider covering it — a single segment on your show or column, situating the bill in the broader accountability conversation you're already part of, would put this in front of an audience that needs to know it exists."*
- **For attorneys**, lean on the optional case-citation paragraph and replace *The ask* item one with: *"Read it with the eye of a working attorney and tell me what's wrong, weak, vulnerable to preemption, or worth tightening — your professional review would substantially strengthen this."*
- **For civic and faith organizations**, keep the healing/unity frame and add a sentence at the end: *"I'd value any nonpartisan engagement your organization is in a position to offer — a discussion at a meeting, a study group, a public forum, or simply circulating the bill to members who would find it relevant."*
- **For citizens in other states**, add at the end: *"If after reading this you find that you'd want to see this bill adopted in [STATE], please email me at citizens.checkmate@gmail.com with subject `CHECKMATE — [STATE NAME]` and I will set up a state-specific branch of the repository tailored for [STATE]. State branches are coordinated centrally under the same canonical project so the work done in one state strengthens the work in every other. Every state that adopts a version of this bill makes the next adoption easier."*
- **For family, friends, and neighbors**, replace the heavy intro paragraphs with a shorter, warmer opening — e.g.: *"You know I've been working on something for a while. This is what it is. I'd love your honest reaction."* — then keep the chess and discipline-gap metaphors and the ask.

Keep the bill links the same in every version. Keep the *non-partisan, citizen-written, no PAC* disclaimer in every version. Those two things travel with the bill regardless of audience.

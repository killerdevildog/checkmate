# How the Trump Administration's Words and Actions Support This Bill

## A Note on What This Document Is — and What It Is Not

This document is not an endorsement of Operation Checkmate by President Donald J. Trump. It is not a claim that the Trump administration has read, reviewed, or expressed any position on the bill. Neither President Trump nor any official of his administration has, to the knowledge of the bill's drafters, taken a public position on Operation Checkmate.

What this document *is* is an observation: that a number of positions the Trump administration has publicly stated, executive actions it has taken, and structural critiques of the federal government it has articulated **align with the operative premises of this bill**. The alignment is structural — meaning the bill's substantive provisions enforce, at the level of state criminal law, the very kinds of constraints on government conduct that the Trump administration's stated positions have repeatedly called for.

This document presents that alignment, with the actual words and actions documented, so that readers — particularly readers who identify with or support the Trump administration's political coalition — can evaluate whether the bill is consistent with the principles they already hold.

A separate and equally important point follows from the same structural premise: **the bill applies to the Trump administration exactly as it applies to any other administration**. The bill is non-partisan in operation. Conduct that violates the substantive provisions of this Act is reachable under the Act regardless of who performs it, regardless of which party they belong to, regardless of which administration they serve, regardless of the political popularity of either the violator or the victim. Where the Trump administration's stated principles match the bill's structural requirements, the bill is consistent with those principles. Where any administration — past, present, or future, including the Trump administration — engages in conduct that violates the bill's substantive provisions, the bill reaches that conduct on the same terms it would reach any other administration's conduct.

This is not, in other words, a partisan document. It is a structural document, oriented toward a particular political coalition, demonstrating that the bill's principles are consistent with positions members of that coalition have publicly endorsed. The same kind of document exists, or could exist, for citizens who identify with other political coalitions — the existing community guides for Black citizens (`culture/blacklivesmatter.md`), LGBTQ+ citizens (`culture/lgbtq.md`), constitutional conservatives and libertarians (`culture/righists.md`), and citizens in financial hardship (`culture/financial_hardship.md`) all show the same kind of cross-spectrum structural alignment. The bill, by design, has reasons for citizens of every political identity to support it. This document develops the reasons specific to citizens who support the Trump administration's stated positions on government accountability.

## 1. "Drain the Swamp" — The Foundational Structural Critique

The earliest and most enduring statement of the Trump administration's structural critique of the federal government is the phrase **"drain the swamp."** First introduced as a campaign theme in October 2016, repeated continuously through the first administration, and reaffirmed throughout the second-term campaign and administration, the "swamp" framing identifies a specific structural condition:

> Powerful actors within the federal government, the regulatory apparatus, the contractor class, and the political-media establishment have, over decades, accumulated power and protection beyond what their formal offices would legitimately confer; they operate as a self-protecting class; they are accountable to citizens only nominally; and the result is a government that serves itself rather than the citizens it was instituted to serve.

The structural critique embedded in "drain the swamp" is exactly the critique Operation Checkmate addresses. The bill's premise — that the immunity architecture has converted public office into an unaccountable form of personal status, that the citizen has been pushed from the center of the constitutional order to its margin, and that the structural fix is to restore accountability through criminal and civil consequences applied equally to officials and citizens — is the same structural diagnosis "drain the swamp" articulates.

The bill is, in operative terms, the legal mechanism the "drain the swamp" critique calls for. A government class that operates above the law cannot be removed from above the law by exhortation; it can only be removed by the application of law that reaches the conduct. The bill applies that law.

## 2. "Two-Tier Justice System" — The Critique the Bill Directly Addresses

Among the most-repeated phrases of the Trump administration and its political coalition has been the critique of the **"two-tier justice system"** — the observation that the federal criminal-justice apparatus has, in operative reality, prosecuted some categories of conduct vigorously when committed by some categories of actors, and not when committed by others. The phrase has been deployed in connection with:

- The differential treatment of January 6 defendants compared to other categories of politically-charged defendants
- The Hunter Biden investigations and their procedural trajectory
- The Mar-a-Lago search and the trajectory of the federal documents prosecution
- The treatment of Trump administration officials compared to officials of other administrations
- The federal investigations of conservative political organizations, parental-rights advocates, and other politically-charged categories
- The differential application of federal civil-rights statutes

Whatever the merits of the specific factual claims in any particular case, **the structural critique embedded in the "two-tier justice" framing is exactly what Operation Checkmate addresses**. The bill's premise is that the same rules must apply to every government actor regardless of office, party, or rank, in the same way the same rules apply to every citizen — and that selective enforcement, selective prosecution, and differential application of the law to politically-favored actors are themselves substantive violations reachable under the Act.

The bill names this conduct directly:

- **§1.1.8 (Obstruction of a Private Citizen)** — including selective non-prosecution of a favored official for conduct that would be prosecuted in an ordinary citizen
- **§1.1.32 (Pretextualism)** — including the use of legal-process cover for political targeting
- **§1.1.6 (Cabalism)** — including coordinated official conduct producing politically-motivated unjust effects
- **§1.1.31 (Aristocratism)** — including the operation of public office as a noble class with different rules than apply to citizens
- **§1.1.14 (Malicious Prosecution)** — including the initiation or continuation of prosecution after the prosecutor knows or should know the target is innocent

A government actor who participates in two-tier justice — applying one rule to favored persons and another rule to disfavored persons — is reachable under the Act on multiple cumulative theories. The bill is the structural answer to the two-tier-justice critique: it forces one tier, by making selective application itself a criminal offense.

## 3. "Weaponization of Government" — Naming Conduct the Act Reaches

The Trump administration has, throughout both terms, repeatedly articulated the critique that **the federal government has been "weaponized"** against political opponents, dissenting citizens, and disfavored institutions. The critique has been deployed in connection with:

- The FBI's investigations of parental-rights advocates following the 2021 DOJ memorandum on threats to school officials
- The FBI's Richmond memorandum on traditional Catholics
- The targeting of religious-liberty cases
- The targeting of conservative tax-exempt organizations (the IRS scandal)
- "Operation Choke Point" under prior administrations
- The Mar-a-Lago search
- The January 6 prosecutorial response
- Federal regulatory action against firearms manufacturers, dealers, and rights organizations
- Federal civil-rights enforcement against pro-life sidewalk counselors

Whatever one's view of any specific incident, **the structural concept of governmental weaponization is exactly what the bill names and reaches**. Government conduct that uses the apparatus of public power to harm citizens — particularly citizens whose only "offense" is dissent from official policy or membership in a politically-disfavored category — is the operative core of what the Act criminalizes.

The bill names the structural offenses:

- **§1.1.12 (Terrorism — Government Context)** — government conduct using state authority to terrorize citizens
- **§1.1.13 (Official Intimidation)** — government conduct producing unjustified anxiety and fear in citizens through the apparatus of office
- **§1.1.32 (Pretextualism)** — government use of pretextual legal or regulatory cover for actually-motivated harm
- **§1.1.42 (Censorism)** — government conduct restricting protected speech
- **§17.2 (Institutional Retaliation)** — government retaliation against citizens for the lawful exercise of rights

The Trump administration's "weaponization" critique identifies the conduct; the bill provides the structural answer — making the conduct itself a Class 1 felony or capital offense reachable by the citizens against whom it is used.

In the second Trump administration, the President's January 20, 2025 executive order titled "Ending the Weaponization of the Federal Government" (E.O. 14147) directed the Attorney General and other officials to review prior administrations' conduct for evidence of weaponization. The Act provides what executive review alone cannot — the substantive criminal law that makes the conduct reachable at the state level, with citizen-driven enforcement that does not depend on federal prosecutorial discretion. The executive review identifies what was done. The Act provides the legal mechanism for accountability where the conduct affected citizens within a state's jurisdiction.

## 4. Executive Orders on Federal Accountability — Operating in the Same Direction as the Act

The Trump administration, in both terms, has issued a series of executive orders aimed at federal accountability, regulatory restraint, and government transparency. The substantive direction of these orders parallels the substantive direction of Operation Checkmate.

**E.O. on Federal Employee Accountability (Schedule F / Schedule Policy/Career)** — The October 2020 executive order creating "Schedule F," reissued and expanded in the second Trump administration, reclassifies federal employees in policy-influencing positions to make them removable for performance and conduct deficiencies that career civil service protections had previously made unreachable. The structural premise is that federal employees exercising substantive policy authority should be accountable to the elected executive branch and, through it, to the citizens — not insulated from accountability by procedural protections that the founders did not contemplate. **This is the same structural premise that animates the Act's removal of the immunity doctrines.** The Act applies the principle at the state level and to a broader category of government actors; the executive orders apply it within the federal civil service. Both operate in the same direction.

**E.O. on Government Transparency and Inspector General Independence** — Various executive orders from the Trump administration have called for greater transparency in federal-agency conduct, expanded reporting requirements, and protections for inspectors general against retaliation. The Act's substantive provisions reach concealment of official conduct (§1.1.8 Obstruction; §1.1.4 Infraction patterns of records destruction; §2.3 Representation of Truth), institutional retaliation against citizens and officials raising concerns (§17.2 Institutional Retaliation), and the kinds of process-laundering that defeat transparency in operative reality (§1.1.3.1 No Process-Laundering Defense). Where the executive orders aim at transparency through administrative direction, the Act provides the substantive criminal-law backstop.

**E.O. on Free Speech and Religious Liberty** — Trump-administration executive orders have repeatedly affirmed federal commitment to free speech, religious liberty, and protection of speakers from governmental retaliation. The Act's §1.1.42 (Censorism), §1.1.36 (Sectarianism), §1.1.32 (Pretextualism), and §17.2 (Institutional Retaliation) make the conduct that violates these commitments reachable as state criminal offenses.

**E.O. on Civil Service Reform / DOGE / Restoring American Workforce Accountability** — The Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), established by E.O. 14158 (January 20, 2025), has been directed to identify and reduce inefficiency, waste, and unaccountable conduct across federal agencies. The Act addresses the same structural concern at the level of substantive criminal law — making unaccountable conduct itself reachable, rather than just inefficient. DOGE identifies; the Act criminalizes. Both operate against the same structural problem: the accumulation of unaccountable power in federal agencies.

## 5. Pardons and Commutations — Direct Statements About Prosecutorial Overreach

President Trump has, in both administrations, used the pardon power to grant clemency to individuals he and his administration characterized as victims of politicized or overreaching federal prosecution. Among the most prominent:

- **The January 6 defendants** — On the first day of the second administration (January 20, 2025), the President issued pardons or commutations for approximately 1,500 individuals convicted or facing prosecution in connection with the January 6, 2021 events. The proclamation explicitly characterized many of the prosecutions as politically motivated and disproportionate.
- **Military personnel** — Across both administrations, the President has pardoned military personnel he characterized as victims of overreaching military justice prosecutions, including Eddie Gallagher, Clint Lorance, and others.
- **Individuals prosecuted under Operation Choke Point–era enforcement** — pardons issued for individuals targeted under what the administration characterized as politicized financial enforcement.
- **Tax-related cases the administration characterized as overcharging** — various commutations and pardons.
- **The Roger Stone, Paul Manafort, and Michael Flynn cases** — characterized by the administration as politicized prosecutions of political allies.

Whatever one's view of any particular pardon, **the underlying premise of these clemency actions is that the federal prosecutorial apparatus has, in specific cases, exceeded the bounds of legitimate criminal-law enforcement and engaged in politically-motivated overreach**. That is precisely the conduct Operation Checkmate's §1.1.14 (Malicious Prosecution) reaches. The bill's substantive offense is the kind of conduct the pardons identify.

A bill that makes malicious or politically-motivated prosecution itself a substantive criminal offense reaches the prosecutorial conduct directly — providing accountability for the prosecutors who engage in it, rather than only providing relief (through clemency) to the citizens they prosecuted. The Trump administration's pardons identify the harm; the Act provides the structural remedy that prevents it from happening in the first place.

## 6. The Critique of Federal Investigative Apparatus — Aligned With the Act's Restraints on Investigative Conduct

The Trump administration and its political coalition have repeatedly critiqued federal investigative conduct in specific cases — the 2016–2020 Russia investigation, the Mar-a-Lago search, the January 6 investigation, various federal grand-jury practices, the alleged improper use of FISA, and the deployment of federal agents in politically-charged contexts. The structural critique embedded in these specific complaints includes:

- Excessive force and disproportionate display of authority in federal raids and searches
- Use of federal grand-jury secrecy to insulate politically-motivated investigations from scrutiny
- Selective leaking of investigative information to harm targets
- Coordination between federal investigators and political actors
- Federal investigative conduct that operates in disregard of constitutional protections

The Act's substantive provisions reach this conduct directly:

- **§1.1.3 (Battery, including §1.1.3.1 No Process-Laundering Defense)** — reaches federal investigative conduct that effects unlawful deprivation of liberty, family, property, or rights against Utah citizens, including conduct laundered through process or chain of officials
- **§1.1.3.3 (Emergency Exigency; Citizen-Defender Presumption)** — reaches federal investigative force where the citizen was exercising self-defense or where the exigency was not actual
- **§1.1.12 (Terrorism)** — reaches federal investigative conduct that targets the citizen's wellbeing in any of the enumerated categories
- **§1.1.13 (Official Intimidation)** — reaches federal investigative conduct producing unjustified anxiety and fear
- **§1.1.46 (Judicial Imposter)** — reaches federal investigative actors making adjudicative-style determinations against citizens without independent judicial review
- **§14.7 (Federal Officer Status Not a Shield)** — explicitly preserves Utah's jurisdiction over federal officers whose conduct violates the Act's provisions against Utah citizens

The Act, in operative effect, provides what the Trump administration's critique calls for: a substantive legal mechanism reaching federal investigative conduct that exceeds constitutional bounds. Where the administration's critique identifies the conduct, the Act provides the remedy. The two operate in the same direction.

## 7. The Substantive Provisions of the Act That Apply to the Trump Administration the Same Way

It is essential to be direct about the symmetry of the Act. The same provisions that align with the Trump administration's stated critiques of prior-administration conduct apply, with equal force, to the conduct of the Trump administration itself.

- **§1.1.14 (Malicious Prosecution)** applies to any federal prosecutor — Biden-administration, Trump-administration, or any other — who knowingly continues a prosecution after evidence of innocence emerges. The Act does not exempt Trump-administration prosecutors any more than it exempts prosecutors of any other administration.
- **§1.1.32 (Pretextualism)** applies to any federal investigation, search, or charging decision motivated by political purpose under a pretextual legal cover, regardless of which administration directs it.
- **§1.1.46 (Judicial Imposter)** applies to executive-branch officials performing judicial functions without independent judicial review, regardless of the administration that empowered them.
- **§1.1.3 (Battery, including §1.1.3.1)** applies to federal raids, searches, and use of force conducted under any administration, including the Trump administration's own immigration enforcement, federal-law-enforcement deployments, and other coercive operations affecting Utah citizens.
- **§1.1.47 (Carceralism)** applies to federal detention, immigration enforcement, and prosecutorial response to citizens in crisis, regardless of administration.

The Act is, in operative reality, the same instrument applied to every administration. Where the Trump administration's stated principles call for restraint on government conduct, the bill enforces that restraint. Where the Trump administration itself engages in conduct the bill reaches, the bill reaches that conduct on the same terms. The structural symmetry is not a defect; it is the operative content of what "the same rules apply to everyone" actually means.

This is why the bill is consistent with the Trump administration's stated principles without being an endorsement of any specific Trump-administration conduct. The structural principles align; the operative reach is universal.

## 8. The Cross-Spectrum Convergence

The alignment between the Trump administration's stated positions and Operation Checkmate is part of a broader cross-spectrum convergence the bill is built around.

- **Citizens on the political right** — through critiques of the "deep state," "weaponization of government," "two-tier justice," federal regulatory overreach, prosecutorial politicization, the immunity architecture protecting officials who violated constitutional rights, and federal-law-enforcement conduct against politically-disfavored citizens.
- **Citizens on the political left** — through critiques of qualified immunity protecting police officers who killed unarmed citizens, prosecutorial misconduct producing wrongful convictions, mass incarceration produced by carceral-first responses to citizen distress, the wealth-based two-tier justice that the cash-bail system and civil forfeiture have produced, and selective enforcement against minority communities.
- **Citizens on the libertarian / constitutionalist tradition** — through critiques of administrative-state expansion, federal-power encroachment on state authority, immunity doctrines that hollow out the Bill of Rights, and the absence of meaningful judicial review of executive determinations.
- **Citizens in the religious-liberty and parental-rights coalitions** — through critiques of federal-investigative conduct against Catholics, evangelicals, parental-rights advocates, and pro-life organizers.
- **Citizens in the Black, Latino, immigrant, LGBTQ+, and other historically-targeted communities** — through critiques of selective enforcement, qualified-immunity-shielded police violence, family-separation policies, and the carceral-first response to citizens in poverty or crisis.

Each of these political identities, from radically different starting points, articulates a critique that the bill addresses structurally. The bill does not require any political coalition to abandon its identity, its priorities, or its political fights. It asks for one thing: that the structural conditions that have made government accountability fail across the board — the immunity architecture, the gatekeeper class, the procedural cover for substantive harm — be reached by a single, equal-application criminal-law instrument.

The Trump administration's alignment with the bill is one example of this cross-spectrum convergence. The bill does not require Trump-administration endorsement. It is consistent with the stated principles of the Trump administration's political coalition while being equally consistent with the stated principles of other coalitions, and equally applicable in operative reality to any administration's conduct.

1. **The structural critiques the Trump administration has articulated** — drain the swamp, two-tier justice, weaponization of government, prosecutorial overreach, federal-investigative excess, unaccountable bureaucratic power — **identify real conditions that have harmed real citizens.**
2. **Operation Checkmate is the structural legal mechanism that addresses those conditions** — at the state level, with criminal-law consequences for the conduct the critiques name, with citizen-driven enforcement that does not depend on the federal prosecutorial discretion the critiques themselves identify as captured.

## 10. Closing Note

This document, like every document in this project, will be read by future citizens — and possibly by future government actors — long after the specific political circumstances of 2025–2026 have passed. The Trump administration's specific positions, executive orders, pardons, and political postures will, with time, become historical fact rather than current event. The structural principles the administration articulated, and which the bill enforces, will remain.

The point of the document is not to anchor the bill to any particular political figure. The point is to demonstrate, with documented evidence, that the bill's structural principles have the support — explicit in some cases, implicit in others — of citizens across the political spectrum, including citizens whose principal frame for government accountability is the framework the Trump administration has articulated. The same demonstration could be made, and is made elsewhere in this project, for citizens whose principal frame is the framework of the Black civil-rights movement, the libertarian tradition, the religious-liberty coalition, the parental-rights movement, the labor movement, the disability-rights movement, or any other framework grounded in the protection of citizens from government conduct that exceeds constitutional limits.

The bill is for all of them. The principles it enforces are the principles the constitutional order itself enforces. The Trump administration's stated positions are one expression of those principles. The bill is the legal mechanism by which the principles operate, regardless of which administration is in office, and regardless of which political coalition the administration represents.

The same rules apply to everyone. That is the constitutional principle. That is what Operation Checkmate enforces. And that is what every coalition — including the Trump administration's coalition — that has supported the principle in some form should, on consistency alone, support in its operative legal form.

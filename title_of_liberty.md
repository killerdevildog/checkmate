# The Title of Liberty

> "And it came to pass that Moroni, who was the chief commander of the armies of the Nephites … took a piece of his garment, and wrote upon it — *In memory of our God, our religion, and freedom, and our peace, our wives, and our children* — and he fastened it upon the end of a pole. And he fastened on his head-plate, and his breastplate, and his shields, and girded on his armor about his loins; and he took the pole, which had on the end thereof his rent coat, (and he called it the title of liberty)."
>
> — Alma 46:12–13

This document is the **title of liberty** of Operation Checkmate. It explains what the bill is in the register that, for me and for many of the people the bill is written to serve, matters most: not as a piece of legal architecture, not as a citizen project on GitHub, not as a state statute proposed for adoption — though it is all of those — but as a **banner of hope**, lifted by ordinary citizens, in memory of those for whom the present condition of government has cost too much.

---

## Why a Title of Liberty

When Captain Moroni took up the rent piece of his garment and wrote on it the things he and his people would not let perish — *God, religion, freedom, peace, wives, children* — he was not declaring war. He was raising a remembrance. He was making, with cloth and a pole, a covenant visible. He gathered to himself the people who would maintain the cause of liberty, and they came forth running, rending their own garments as a token of the same covenant (Alma 46:18–21).

A banner is not a weapon. A banner is a *memory made visible*. It tells everyone who passes beneath it what is being defended, who is doing the defending, and what they will not allow to be lost. Moroni's banner did not promise that no enemy would attack. It did not promise that no one would fall. It promised that those who fell would fall for something named — for things written on the cloth — and that as long as the banner remained lifted, the cause it named would not be forgotten.

Operation Checkmate is, in its operative legal form, a proposed state accountability statute. It abrogates immunity doctrines. It criminalizes obviously-wrongful official conduct. It restores citizen power to enforce. Those things are explained in the bill text and in the supporting documents in this repository. But this document, at the root of the project, is not the bill. **It is the banner the bill is fastened to.** It tells anyone who walks past who the bill is for, who built it, and what they will not allow to be lost.

## The names on the cloth

In memory of —

- **My family** — those who taught me, before I had the language for it, that the law is supposed to apply to everyone the same way; that an honest man does not hide behind his office; and that a country worth living in is one in which the people in power are bound by the same rules as the people they govern.

- **My loved ones** — those who have stood beside me, prayed with me, encouraged me, corrected me, and trusted me with the truth about their own encounters with a government that did not love them back. Their stories are in the substance of this bill, even where their names are not.

- **My community** — the citizens of my city, my county, my state, and my country, regardless of party, faith, race, or station — every one of whom has at some point felt the difference between the rules they live under and the rules the people in power live under, and every one of whom deserves the dignity of a government that is bound to the same standard they are.

- **Those who have died for this country** — soldiers, sailors, airmen, marines, guardsmen, and every other servicemember who has fallen in service of the Constitution they swore to defend; first responders who fell protecting the citizens they served; citizens who fell at the hands of an official who would never have answered for it under the architecture that has been allowed to stand. Their oaths and their sacrifices were not made to a government that would exempt itself from the law. They were made to a Constitution that binds every officer of the United States, in every office, equally to the citizens it was written to protect. This bill is one citizen's attempt to make the country they died for live up to the country they believed they were dying for.

> *In memory of our God, our religion, and freedom, and our peace, our wives, and our children.*

## The condition of the present

Two thousand years ago, on a different continent, a different people watched their republic fail because a secret combination of judges, lawyers, and officials, sworn in oath to one another rather than to their citizens, captured the machinery of government and used it to protect their own. The scriptural record of that failure is among the most pointed political warnings ever set down:

> "Yea, well doth he cry unto you … that ye are united unto that band of robbers, the Gadianton robbers; yea, and do let the guilty and the wicked go unpunished because of their money; and moreover to be retained in office at the head of government, to rule and do according to their wills, that they might get gain and glory of the world, and, moreover, that they might the more easily commit adultery, and steal, and kill, and do according to their own wills."
>
> — Helaman 7:4–5

> "And it came to pass on the other hand, that the Nephites did build them up and support them, beginning at the more wicked part of them, until they had overspread all the land of the Nephites, and had seduced the more part of the righteous until they had come down to believe in their works and partake of their spoils, and to join with them in their secret murders and combinations."
>
> — Helaman 6:38

> "Wherefore, the Lord commandeth you, when ye shall see these things come among you that ye shall awake to a sense of your awful situation, because of this secret combination which shall be among you …"
>
> — Ether 8:24

The parallel between the Gadianton robbers and the present condition of American government is not metaphor. It is mechanism. Judges, prosecutors, and officers who have been allowed, through the immunity architecture, to police themselves and protect one another from accountability — letting the guilty among them "go unpunished because of their money," retained in office to "rule and do according to their wills" — are operating, as a structural matter, the same way the Gadianton robbers operated: by oath sworn to one another and to the protection of their own conduct, rather than to the citizens whose rights they were entrusted to defend. The mechanism is the same. The warning is the same. The condition the warning was given against is the condition the present generation has inherited.

The scriptures do not say to despair when this condition appears. They say to *awake to a sense of your awful situation* (Ether 8:24) — and they make clear that the awakening, and the response, is the responsibility of the citizens who see it. The robbers did not destroy the Nephites by force of arms. They destroyed them by being tolerated in office while citizens carried on with their lives and assumed someone else would address it. The destruction was complete when "the more part of the righteous … come down to believe in their works and partake of their spoils." The warning is against the apathy that allows the combination to spread, as much as against the combination itself.

Operation Checkmate is a citizen instrument written in answer to that warning. It is not the destruction of any office. It does not call for vengeance, for armed action, for any conduct outside the law. It does precisely what Moroni's title of liberty did: it gathers the citizens who will maintain the cause of liberty under a written remembrance, and it gives them a lawful, peaceful, openly-published instrument by which the secret combinations operating in our day can be brought, through the rules of the constitutional order itself, into the light. The king is brought to a stop by the lawful rules of the game, openly and in full view of both players. That is the principle. That is the instrument. That is the banner.

## What this banner says

The cloth lifted by this bill is written with the same care Moroni wrote with on his.

- That **the same rules apply to everyone, regardless of office** — the principle the Constitution promised, the principle the Reconstruction Amendments extended, the principle the immunity architecture has hollowed out, and the principle this bill restores.

- That **no person, office, agency, badge, robe, title, or institution stands above the citizen whose rights government exists to protect** — the principle this bill names and writes into law.

- That **citizens are part of the constitutional order, not bystanders to it** — that the Citizen Council mechanism, by which twelve or more citizens may convene to investigate official misconduct and act where the prosecutorial gatekeepers will not, is the present-day instrument by which the principle of citizen sovereignty becomes operative again.

- That **the country we live in can become the country the people we love died believing it was** — that the gap between the oath as taken and the law as practiced is a gap citizens can close, lawfully, peacefully, and openly, by the same constitutional means our founders built into the architecture from the beginning.

- That **the secret combinations of our day are not invincible**, that the principle which has stood from Magna Carta through the Founders through the Reconstruction generation to the present is the principle the Lord commanded us to awake to and address, and that the awakening is here, now, in the form of citizens lifting a banner and writing on it the things we will not let perish.

---

## What this banner is not

It is not a flag of any party. It is not a flag of any faction. It is not a call to violence, to armed action, to militia organization, to extra-legal conduct of any kind. Operation Checkmate is a peaceful, lawful, openly-published citizen statute, designed to operate entirely within the constitutional order it is written to defend. It rejects, explicitly and in the bill's own text, every framing that would turn it into a weapon rather than an instrument. Moroni's banner was raised not to begin a war but to gather the people who would defend, lawfully and together, what they had been entrusted to defend. This banner is raised in the same spirit.

It is not the property of any single citizen, including the one drafting these words. The bill is published in the open, on a public repository, available for forking, adaptation, criticism, refinement, and adoption in every state. The principle the banner names — *that the same rules apply to everyone* — belongs to no party, no faction, no movement, no individual. It belongs to the people the Constitution was written to protect, in every state of the Union, regardless of who they vote for or what they believe.

It is not finished. A banner does not have to be finished to be raised. The bill will be improved by every citizen who reads it, criticizes it, files an issue, opens a pull request, or sits down at a kitchen table with a neighbor and walks through what it would mean for their family. The banner is lifted now precisely so that the people who would gather under it can begin gathering.

## In every household

This bill belongs in every household in America.

Not on the shelves of law libraries alone. Not in the offices of constitutional scholars alone. Not in the briefcases of attorneys and the chambers of judges alone. In every household — printed, read, discussed at kitchen tables, taught to children, kept beside the Constitution and the Bill of Rights as a present-day instrument of the same inheritance.

The Constitution and the Bill of Rights were written for citizens. They were printed in newspapers, debated in pamphlets, read aloud in town halls, carried in the saddlebags of farmers and shopkeepers and tradesmen, and discussed by name in households across the colonies. The Federalist Papers were written not for a guild of professionals but for ordinary readers of the *Independent Journal* and the *New-York Packet*. The founders did not envision the lawyer, the judge, or the career official as the exclusive custodian of constitutional meaning. They envisioned the citizen — the head of household, the parent, the apprentice, the soldier, the farmer, the merchant, the schoolteacher — as the proper custodian. "We the People … do ordain and establish this Constitution" was not an honorific. It was a description of where the constitutional authority of this country actually lives, and where the founders intended it to remain.

> *"To secure the blessings of liberty to ourselves and our posterity."* — Preamble, U.S. Constitution.

Posterity. Not the posterity of the legal profession. Not the posterity of the political class. The posterity of every citizen — of every household — of the very people in whose name the document was written and ratified in the first place.

What we have inherited instead is not what they meant for us. The constitutional order they built has, over generations, been so far hollowed out that ordinary citizens have stopped believing the law applies to those who govern. Judges shield judges. Prosecutors shield prosecutors. Officers shield officers. The doctrines that authorize this have been written, decade by decade, in language no ordinary citizen was expected to be able to read — by a professional class that has, in effect, gatekept the meaning of the Constitution away from the very people the Constitution was written to protect. The result is the disorder of the present moment. Not an accident. Not bad luck. Not the inevitable consequence of complexity. **The direct consequence of allowing the constitutional inheritance of every American household to be confiscated, paragraph by paragraph, by a class that exempted itself from the rules it was meant to administer.**

This is the chaos we have inherited. This is what generations of citizens have been told, in effect, they should sit quietly and tolerate, because constitutional questions are too complicated for ordinary people to understand. This is the condition the founders explicitly warned against. And this is exactly the condition the title of liberty was raised, two thousand years ago in scriptural memory and again now in this document, to address.

Operation Checkmate is written in plain language for that reason. It is published, in full, in a public repository for that reason. It is offered as a free PDF that fits in any household device for that reason. The companion guides, the FAQ, the community-specific letters — every supporting document in this project is written so that an ordinary citizen, without legal training, can read it, understand it, discuss it with their family, and decide for themselves whether it deserves their support. **The constitutional inheritance the founders left for their posterity was never supposed to require permission from a professional class to be read. This bill is offered in that same spirit, and it belongs in the same place that inheritance was always meant to live — in the hands of the household, the head of household, the parent, the spouse, the child old enough to read, and the neighbor across the fence.**

Print it. Read it aloud. Discuss it at the dinner table. Teach it to your children alongside the Constitution and the Declaration. Keep a copy of the PDF on every device in your house. Carry the principle into every conversation you have about why the country is in the condition it is in, and what an ordinary citizen, ordinary household, ordinary community can do about it. The founders meant for the standard of liberty to be a household possession. We have been allowed, over generations, to forget that. The remedy begins the moment a citizen remembers — and lifts the banner in the place where civic memory is either preserved or lost.

The standard of liberty was meant to be raised in every household in America. It was meant to be the inheritance of every citizen, in every state, regardless of party, faith, race, or station. It was meant to be the answer to the question every parent eventually has to answer for their child: *what kind of country are we leaving you?* The founders had an answer to that question. The present moment has, in too many ways, betrayed it. **This bill is one citizen's attempt to give every household in this country the instrument by which the founders' answer can be made true again.**

## A covenant, plainly stated

In the spirit of Alma 46, and in the spirit of every American citizen who has ever sworn an oath to the Constitution — soldier, sailor, airman, marine, officer, judge, clerk, juror, neighbor, parent — this banner names what it covenants to defend:

- The Constitution, federal and state, as written.
- The principle that public power is held in trust for the people, not as the personal property of those entrusted with it.
- The dignity and rights of every citizen, regardless of party, faith, race, station, or station of the official they are dealing with.
- The memory and the sacrifice of those who have died believing this country was something better than the corrupt machinery it has, in too many cases, been allowed to become.
- The future of the children of every citizen of this country, who deserve to inherit a republic in which the people in power are bound by the same rules as the people they govern.

And it covenants, finally, to do the work — peacefully, lawfully, in the open, alongside every citizen of whatever conviction who will gather under this banner — until the principle named on it is written into the law of every state, and the secret combinations of our day are brought, by the rules of the constitutional order itself, into the light.
